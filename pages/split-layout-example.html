History

The Arena di Verona is a Roman amphitheatre built in the 1st century AD. It's one of the best-preserved ancient structures of its kind and can hold over 15,000 spectators in its 44 tiers of marble seats.

Despite suffering a major earthquake in 1117 that destroyed most of the outer ring, the arena has remained in continuous use throughout the centuries.

Opera Festival

Since 1913, the arena has been the venue for the world-famous Verona Opera Festival, which runs every summer. The acoustics and atmosphere make it one of the most spectacular opera venues in the world.

The arena is located in the heart of Verona's historic center in Piazza Bra, the city's largest square. It's easily accessible on foot from anywhere in the old town.

Tickets for opera performances should be booked well in advance, especially for popular shows. Daytime tours of the arena are also available.

Festival of the dead

Every year on November 2nd, Verona hosts the "Festival of the Dead" (Festa dei Morti), a unique cultural event that combines historical reenactments, theatrical performances, and guided tours of the city's ancient cemeteries. The festival pays homage to the city's rich history and its connection to the afterlife, drawing visitors from around the world.
